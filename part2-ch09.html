IX

The Claiming

After, she slept on his chest.

The claiming had left them in a bed the cathedral had made of dark and petals, his wings a canopy, her white hair spilled over the ink that had outlived empires. Asmodeus did not sleep. He watched the red move across her new horns and thought of the willow, and the blood, and the promise he had made with an open palm.

What the water did not wash away sat in the room with them.

Seraphine’s light was quieter than it had been at the threshold. Not gone. Thinned. As if the red had taken a tithe for every hour she had been allowed to walk here.

He could have woken her.

He did not.

Vespera’s absence was its own shape on the other side of the dark. He felt it the way he felt every unfinished hunt. For the first time in a very long time, keeping them both felt like a math he might not be able to finish.
