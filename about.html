Rainbow Rot

The House

It is not a building.
It is the shape the story takes when it decides to stay.

Every room holds a different weight of the claim.

The first threshold is the Cover — the moment the reader agrees to step inside. Seraphine still crowned in light, Vespera already leaning into the fall, the willow watching. This is the only place the light is still polite.

The long corridor of Chapters is the spine of the house. Ten doors in Part One. Twelve more in Part Two. Each one opens onto a deeper room of the same night. The air grows heavier the farther you walk. By the time you reach the blood and the horns, the walls themselves remember the taste of iron.

The Character rooms sit off the main hall. Public faces on the walls. Private lines written in the dark behind the plaster — the wounds that only surface when the reader lingers too long.

The final chamber is quiet. The three of them still holding one another. Then the withered roses and the fading rainbow. Then the last door that does not close — the Red Places, already breathing crimson light through the cracks.

The House does not protect its inhabitants.
It only keeps them.

It does not offer redemption.
It offers permanence.

Every image is a window.
Every chapter is a room that cannot be left once entered.
Every private line is a crack in the foundation that lets the older dark come in.

This is why we call it the House.

Not because it shelters.
Because once you step across the threshold, the story begins to own the shape of your absence the same way the claim owns theirs.

The walls are listening.
They always have been.
