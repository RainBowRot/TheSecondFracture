XII

The Long Way Back

The red would not let her leave as she was.

They reached the flooded heart of the cathedral and the water took the last of her borrowed solidity. Pearl wings went to light, then to motes, then to the same drifting red that had greeted them at the threshold. Seraphine lay back in his arms and looked up at him with a calm the Garden had never taught her.

“It was always going to be this,” she said.

He held her face as if stone could be persuaded.

The long way back was not a path. It was a refusal. Asmodeus gathered what the water had not yet unmade — the heat of her, the last of the gold that was not gold, the weight of a name — and he did not ask the red for mercy. He took.

Vespera found them by the sound he made when there was almost nothing left to hold.

They did not speak of staying.

They spoke of returning.

What they carried out of the red was not a body the Garden would recognize. It was a promise, and a silence, and the two of them.
