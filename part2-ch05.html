The Edge

He found Vespera at the lip of the abyss, where the city burned below them in a slow, patient fire. His hand settled at her waist the way it had in the centuries before Seraphine had a name.

“You brought her here,” he said.

“You offered her the water.”

Neither of them was accusing. They were only telling the truth at the edge of a world that had always belonged to the two of them first.

Vespera looked down into the dark. “The red will not keep her the way it keeps us.”

Asmodeus’s thumb moved once against the lace at her hip. “The bond was meant to let her walk.”

“Walking is not the same as remaining.”

He did not argue. He had seen this cost before, in other mouths, in other years. He had never before wanted so badly to be wrong.

Behind them, far from the edge, Seraphine’s new light still moved through the cathedral like a rumor the stone had not yet decided to keep.
