What the Garden remembers of them.

Vespera

The One Who Fled

Long magenta hair, black curved horns, glowing red eyes. Rose tattoos climb both arms. She wears black lace and gold that never quite looks innocent. Large dark red wings. Body marked by centuries of running and the quiet hunger of someone who has never been allowed to stay.

Demoness of the Red Places. She fled rather than kneel, yet every century since has been spent half-hoping the borders would still refuse her. Returning was never meant to be surrender — until she stood under the willow and discovered that freedom without belonging is only another kind of exile.

She does not know which hunger will win: the one that runs, or the one that stays.

Seraphine

The Guardian Who Opened the Gate

Angel: Long white hair, soft golden halo, pure white feathered wings, ethereal white and gold dress. Clear eyes that once knew only light.

Remade: The same white hair, now crowned with curved pearlescent horns. The halo is gone. Her wings have shifted into iridescent pearl-rose forms. She still wears white, but the light inside her skin has changed.

She was given light and borders and told that wanting was the first fracture. When the demon stepped through the circle she did not raise the wards; she only watched, and felt the clean architecture of her purpose begin to crack.

The horns did not erase the angel. They only made the loss permanent. Every time she answers to the two who remade her, a quieter voice still asks whether belonging this complete is love or the final surrender of the self she was never allowed to keep.

Asmodeus

The Hunter Who Learned to Keep

Tall, powerfully built. Dark wavy hair, black curved horns, glowing red eyes. Intricate black tattoos cover his chest, shoulders, and arms. Large dark red wings. A presence that feels like both threat and shelter.

He spent centuries hunting what fled him, certain that possession would end the ache. When he found her already claimed by light, the old certainty fractured. Now he holds both of them and discovers the new terror: that keeping is not the same as owning, and that the thing he most wants might still choose to leave.

He has never been taught how to want without conquering. He is learning the cost in real time.
