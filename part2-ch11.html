XI

Two Shadows

When the third light is not in the frame, the room becomes a different kind of honest.

He knelt to her. His hand found her throat, her jaw, the wet at the corner of her eye. Seraphine looked up at him as if she were still trying to memorize a border she had already crossed.

“I kept you,” he said.

“I know.”

It was not enough, and they both knew it. Two shadows in a cathedral that had never been built for three. Vespera’s hunger still lived in the bond on Seraphine’s palm, but Vespera was not here to hold her while the red finished its arithmetic.

Seraphine’s fingers rested over his heart.

“If I cannot walk out,” she whispered, “do not let her walk out alone.”

Asmodeus’s face did something almost like breaking.

He had spent centuries claiming. He had never been asked to carry.
