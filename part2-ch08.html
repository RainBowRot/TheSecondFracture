VIII

The Red Water

They went into the water together.

It was not the Garden’s river. It did not remember Lilith. It did not pretend to be kind. It rose around Seraphine’s white silk and Asmodeus’s darker skin and made a drowned chapel of the space between their mouths.

She kissed him as if the water could not have her if he was already there. He lifted her against him, wings cutting the red, and the claiming that had begun under the willow finished in a place that had never learned the word mercy.

Light that was no longer gold moved through the flood.

Darkness that was no longer only hunger answered it.

The water did not wash them clean. It recorded them. Every sound she made, every place his hands learned her, every moment she chose him in a world built to unmake her kind — the red kept it.

Above the flood, in the higher stone, Vespera listened to a silence that was not silence at all.
