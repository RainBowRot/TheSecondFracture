Chapter II

Stolen Hours

They stole time the way other creatures stole breath.

Under the willow, in the spaces between lantern light, Seraphine learned the shape of a mouth that had never been meant to touch hers. Vespera kissed her like someone who had spent centuries practicing the art of taking what was forbidden and making it feel like inevitability. Soft at first. Then deeper. Then claiming.

Seraphine’s hands found the black lace at Vespera’s waist and did not push her away. The halo still burned above her hair. The borders still stood. But something older than either of them had already begun to lean toward the darker gravity of the claim.

“You taste like light that has never been touched,” Vespera murmured against her mouth. “I want to ruin that.”

Seraphine’s answer was a quiet sound that was not refusal.

They stayed like that until the lanterns burned lower and the petals gathered in their hair. Until the sacred clearing no longer felt sacred — only shared. Until the first real hunger woke in the angel’s body and she understood, with a clarity that frightened her, that she would not be the one to end this.

When they finally pulled apart, Vespera’s red eyes were darker. Seraphine’s white wings trembled once, as if they already knew what was coming.

The Garden watched and said nothing.

It was already too late for silence to save any of them.
