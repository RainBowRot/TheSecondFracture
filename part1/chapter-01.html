Chapter I

The Borders Between Us

The Garden still believed it could be kept.

Seraphine walked the borders the way she had for centuries — light in her veins, the wards answering to the quiet authority of her voice. The willow hung heavy with lanterns that never burned out. Petals fell in slow, obedient spirals onto moss that had never known the touch of anything unclean. The waterfall sang the same hymn it had sung since the first dawn. Nothing had ever crossed the red circle and remained.

Until the night the water itself seemed to open.

Vespera stepped through as if the borders had always been a suggestion rather than a law. Magenta hair dark with river water, red eyes already fixed on the angel who should have cast her out. The wards shivered. The light around Seraphine’s skin flared once, then hesitated. She did not raise them. She only watched, and felt the first clean crack run through the architecture of her purpose.

Vespera did not kneel. She never had. She stood in the sacred clearing with water still running down the black lace of her dress and looked at the guardian of the Garden as if she were the one who had been waiting.

“You were supposed to refuse me,” Vespera said. Her voice was low, almost gentle. “Every century I came to the edge and the borders held. Tonight they opened.”

Seraphine’s wings stirred. White. Still pure. Still hers. “I did not open them.”

“No,” Vespera answered. “You simply stopped closing them.”

The silence that followed was not empty. It was heavy with the knowledge that something irreversible had already begun. The lanterns burned a little lower. The petals fell a little faster. And somewhere beyond the circle, in the deep red places Vespera had fled, something ancient and patient felt the first fracture and smiled.

Seraphine did not cast her out.

That was the first sin.
