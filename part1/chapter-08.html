Chapter VIII

The Fall

The gold left her skin like a tide going out.

It began at her fingertips and climbed. The soft radiance that had lived under her flesh for centuries dimmed, then flickered, then withdrew. Seraphine felt it leave the way someone feels a fever break — relief and loss tangled so tightly they became the same sensation.

White horns rose through the pale hair that had once been only light. They pushed upward in slow, irreversible arcs, curved and pearlescent, catching the last of the lantern glow. The halo above her head fractured with a sound like distant glass. Pieces of pure gold light fell into the moss and vanished.

She did not scream.

She only held on to the two who had remade her — one hand locked in Vespera’s magenta hair, the other pressed over the dark ink of Asmodeus’s heart — and let the last of her old name dissolve into the bond that now lived under her ribs.

When it was finished, Seraphine’s wings were no longer purely of heaven. The feathers had taken on an iridescent sheen, white shot through with the faintest rose and pearl. Her eyes, once clear blue, held a new depth. She looked at the two demons who had claimed her and found no fear left in herself. Only belonging.

Vespera kissed the base of one new horn. Asmodeus pressed his forehead to hers.

“You are still you,” he said.

Seraphine’s voice, when it came, was quieter than before, but steadier. “No. I am more.”
