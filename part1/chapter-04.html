Chapter IV

The Serpent Watches

He had hunted her across centuries.

Asmodeus stood at the edge of the clearing, dark wings folded against the night, red eyes reflecting the lantern light, and watched the angel and the demoness forget there had ever been borders. He did not interrupt. He only waited for the moment the claim would open wide enough to hold a third.

Vespera felt him first. She always did. Even with Seraphine’s mouth against her throat and the angel’s light softening around them, she knew the exact second his gaze settled on her spine. She did not pull away from the kiss. She only opened her eyes and looked past Seraphine’s shoulder into the dark.

Asmodeus stepped forward.

The willow did not try to stop him. The wards, already broken for one, made no protest for the second. He stopped a few paces away, tall and patient, the black ink of his tattoos shifting like living things across his chest.

“You found a new light to hide in,” he said quietly to Vespera. His voice was low, almost fond. “I should have known.”

Seraphine turned. The halo above her hair flared once in instinctive warning, then steadied. She did not step between them. She only watched the way Vespera’s body leaned — not away from the angel, but toward the demon who had spent centuries chasing her.

Asmodeus’s gaze moved from Vespera to Seraphine and lingered. Something ancient and hungry stirred behind his red eyes.

“And you,” he said to the angel, “have already started falling.”

The three of them stood in the sacred clearing under the willow, and for the first time the Garden felt too small to hold what was coming.
