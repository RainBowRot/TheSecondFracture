Chapter VI

What We Claim

They claimed each other the way storms claim the shore.

No more hovering at the edge. Seraphine (still haloed, already falling) gave herself to both. Vespera and Asmodeus received her without hesitation. Bodies, mouths, the slow irreversible mathematics of three becoming one closed circle under the green light of the willow.

Asmodeus’s hands were dark against the white of her skin. Vespera’s mouth found the places the light still clung to and kissed them until they dimmed. Seraphine’s wings spread wide, trembling, pure white feathers catching the lantern glow as she arched between them. She did not pray. She only breathed their names like a new kind of devotion.

When the claiming finally broke over them, it was not gentle. It was complete. The bond settled into their blood with the weight of something that had always been waiting. Three heartbeats. One rhythm. The Garden itself seemed to hold its breath.

Afterward they lay tangled in the soft dark, the lanterns burning low, the first true weight of what they had done settling into skin and bone. Nothing had been taken by force. Everything had been given. And the Garden, for the first time, had nothing left to say.
