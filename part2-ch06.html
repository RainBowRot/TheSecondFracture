VI

Kept

Vespera walked the flooded nave alone.

The water came to her knees. The rose window burned at the far end like an eye that had always known her. She had been kept in these halls before — by him, by exile, by the long hunger that had taught her how to survive without being held.

She was crying and did not bother to stop.

Seraphine had taken his hand at the threshold. Seraphine had sat against him as if the red were a language she was already fluent in. Vespera had watched every turn, and the watching had not made her generous.

The Red Places kept what they wanted.

They kept the hunted. They kept the ancient. They did not, in the end, keep borrowed light.

Vespera stood in the water that had once been her only country and understood the second fracture’s quieter price: she had brought the angel she loved into a world that would not know how to give her back.
