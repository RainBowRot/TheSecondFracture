The Correction

He did not raise his voice.

He simply turned and looked at her the way a man looks at a decision he has already made. Seraphine stood in the red light with tears already tracking down her face, one hand pressed to her chest as if she could hold the bond in place by force. The white of her horns and wings still glowed. She still believed, even now, that love could be enough to keep him.

Asmodeus’s voice was quiet. “The claim was never only ours.”

“It can be,” she whispered. “I can make it be.”

“No.”

He moved faster than mercy. One hand closed around her throat — not to harm, but to hold her still while the Red Places answered him. Seraphine’s back met the stone. Her wings flared once in instinctive panic, iridescent and useless. The red light poured through his palm and into her, and the power that had remade her began to unravel.

The horns fractured first. Then the wings. Then the deeper claim that had lived under her skin since the night under the willow. She gasped as the demonic half of her was stripped away, leaving only the fallen angel and the woman who could no longer stay in this place.

Asmodeus’s eyes never left hers. There was no cruelty in them. Only the cold, final clarity of someone correcting a mistake before it destroyed all three of them.

“Go,” he said. His voice was almost gentle. “Find the life that was never supposed to include us.”

Seraphine’s last sound was not a scream.

It was a quiet, broken recognition that she had never been the one he was going to choose.
