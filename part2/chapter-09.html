IX

The Decision

She slept against him as if the claim had never been broken.

Seraphine’s white hair spilled across Asmodeus’s chest. Her hand rested over the dark ink of his heart. Her breathing was soft, trusting, complete. In sleep she still believed the circle of two was enough. That the bond had simply narrowed and not begun to starve.

Asmodeus did not sleep.

He stared into the red dark above them and felt the quiet, relentless truth settle into his bones: the claim that held them was still answering to a third name. No amount of closeness, no amount of blood or water or desperate belonging, had erased it. Vespera’s absence lived under his skin the same way her presence once had. And Seraphine, for all her beauty and all her surrender, could not fill the space that had been shaped for three.

He looked down at the angel who had fallen for him and knew what he would have to do.

The decision did not feel like cruelty.

It felt like the only honesty left.
