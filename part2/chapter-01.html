I

The Red Places

The Red Places did not welcome them.

They simply opened.

Blood-light poured through broken gothic arches. The air itself tasted of iron and old promises. Far below any surface the Garden had ever known, the drowned cathedral waited — black stone, glowing fungi, and a sky that looked like an open wound. This was the place Vespera had fled. This was the place Asmodeus had always ruled. And now Seraphine stood between them, white horns catching the red light, iridescent wings half-folded, and felt the bond in her blood answer the deeper dark.

Asmodeus held out his hand.

Seraphine took it.

Vespera watched the exchange with something sharp and quiet in her red eyes. She had brought them here. She had chosen this. But the moment Seraphine’s fingers closed around Asmodeus’s, something in the demoness shifted — a small, almost invisible fracture that none of them named yet.

“Welcome home,” Asmodeus said. Not to Vespera. To both of them.

The Red Places listened. And for the first time in centuries, they did not feel empty.
