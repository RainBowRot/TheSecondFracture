II

Among the Red

They found a chamber that remembered him.

Seraphine sat against his knee as if the red were easier to bear when it had to pass through his heat first. Her hand rested over the ink on his chest. She looked at him the way she had looked at the Garden in the last hour before the fall — as if she were already saying goodbye to something she had not yet lost.

Vespera stood apart.

This world had never been meant for Seraphine’s light. Vespera had known that when she led them across the water. Knowing did not make it simpler to watch the angel she had stolen from the borders lean into the ancient hunger that had once hunted her through these same halls.

Asmodeus did not send Vespera away.

He also did not call her closer.

The red made a third presence of the space between them, and none of them named it.
