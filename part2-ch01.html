I

The Reaching

Asmodeus reached first.

He had crossed these stones for longer than either of them had been named, and he did not offer his hand as comfort. He offered it as a border. Seraphine looked at it the way she had once looked at the willow — as something that would change her if she touched it.

She took it.

Vespera watched the turn.

The cathedral rose behind them in a sky the color of a cut. What the red used for petals drifted through the heat. Seraphine’s new wings caught the light and threw it back in pale fire. Vespera’s older wings knew this air and did not trust it.

“Stay close,” Asmodeus said.

He did not say to whom.

Seraphine’s fingers closed more tightly around his. Vespera did not reach for either of them. She had come home, and home was already beginning to take.
