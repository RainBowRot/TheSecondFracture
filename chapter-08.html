Chapter VIII

The Fall

It did not happen with thunder.

It happened the way most true falls do — quietly, and too late to take back.

Seraphine felt it first in the light around her. The familiar gold that had always answered her call began to thin, as if the Garden itself was slowly withdrawing what it had once given freely. Her wings still opened. Her halo still glowed. But something essential had already started to leave.

Vespera noticed the change before Seraphine could name it. She reached for her, hands careful on the angel’s face, eyes sharp with a sudden, helpless fear. Asmodeus stood a short distance away, watching with the stillness of a creature who had seen this particular cost before and had never been able to stop it.

Seraphine looked at the two of them — the demon she had chosen and the ancient hunger that had chosen to stay — and understood.

The Garden had not punished her for desire.
It had simply recorded the price.

By staying, by claiming and being claimed, she had stepped outside the borders that had always defined her. What remained of her light was no longer enough to keep her what she had been.

When the last of the gold faded from her skin, Seraphine did not fall to her knees.

She only stood very still in the soft dark under the willow, mortal for the first time, and let Vespera’s arms close around her as if they could hold her in this new, fragile shape.

Asmodeus did not speak.

But he did not leave.

The Garden kept its silence.

And the second fracture was complete.
