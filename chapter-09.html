Chapter IX

Blood & Promise

Seraphine was mortal now.

The knowledge sat between the three of them like a third presence under the willow. Vespera held her as if she might still disappear. Asmodeus watched them both with the calm of someone who had already calculated the only path left.

“She cannot follow us as she is,” he said at last. His voice was low, almost careful. “The Red Sea will not take what still belongs to the light that was taken from her.”

Vespera’s head lifted sharply. “Then we do not go.”

“Or,” Asmodeus continued, eyes never leaving Seraphine, “we make her something the borders can no longer claim.”

He did not say the word blood. He did not need to. Seraphine understood. The old laws were simple and absolute: what was once divine could be remade only through willing sacrifice and the binding of essence. A bond of blood would tether her to them — to Vespera’s hunger and to his own ancient claim — and in return the last fragile mortal shell would be rewritten.

Seraphine looked at Vespera first.
Then at Asmodeus.

There was no fear in her when she finally spoke.

“Do it.”

Asmodeus’s expression shifted into something almost reverent. He drew a thin line across his own palm, then Vespera’s, then waited. Seraphine offered her hand without hesitation. When their blood met, the Garden itself seemed to hold its breath.

Light that was no longer gold, and darkness that was no longer only hunger, braided together in the space between their joined hands.

The promise was sealed.

Whatever Seraphine became in that moment, she would never again be only mortal — and she would never again be alone.
