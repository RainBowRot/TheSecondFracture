Afterglow

The quiet that follows a fall is never empty.

They stood apart in the nave when she woke. Petals, or what the red used for petals, drifted between them. Seraphine’s hand was at her own throat, as if she needed to be sure the pulse was still hers. There were tears she had not decided to cry.

Asmodeus looked back at her over his shoulder.

He had seen this look on other creatures, in other centuries. He had not wanted it on her.

“It is taking me,” she said.

He did not lie to her. The red places did not bargain. They collected. The bond had opened the water; it had not taught the water how to love an angel, even a remade one.

“Then we leave,” he said.

Seraphine’s mouth tried to be brave and failed in a small, mortal way. “If it will let us.”

Behind the pillars, unseen, Vespera heard them both.
